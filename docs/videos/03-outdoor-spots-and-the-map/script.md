# 03 · Outdoor spots and the map

Studios are half of ShotThere. The other half is outdoor spots: bridges, parks, lanes and public buildings around Toronto. This walkthrough covers the spot list, a spot review, and the map that shows everything at once.

## The spot list

The spot list works like the studio list. Four spots are published in the mocks. Each card shows the type, the neighbourhood, what the spot is best for, and the permit rule for portraits, because that is the first thing a photographer needs to know.

Distillery District is the odd one out. It needs a permit even for portraits, and the card says so.

## Filtering spots

Spots filter by type, park, architecture, waterfront or indoor public, and by use, from engagement and family to brand and video. Here, the visitor picked waterfront, and one spot remains: Humber Bay Arch Bridge. As with studios, the filters live in the link. The MVP has no permit filter yet.

The other states mirror the studio list. While results update, the list says it is loading.

When nothing matches, the message names the gap in our coverage. We haven't reviewed an indoor public spot for family shoots yet.

When the server fails, the filters are kept, and Try again is one click away.

And before any spot is published, the page says why, and points to the studio reviews.

## A spot review

Here is Humber Bay Arch Bridge. The top of the page shows the type and neighbourhood, then a summary panel: the portrait permit rule, what the spot is best for, the review date, and a Get directions button that opens a map in a new tab. On a phone, that panel sits right under the photo.

## Getting there

The location section shows a static map with the exact coordinates and a link to open them in a map app. In the MVP, this is an image, not an interactive map. Below it, the review explains how to get there by transit, by car and on foot.

## Best light and sample shots

Best light is what makes a spot review useful. It is rated for morning, midday, afternoon, golden hour and blue hour, each with a short note. Then a note for each season, like when the willows leaf out, or when the path closes after snow.

Sample shots show what the spot actually looks like through a lens. Each one is labelled with its focal length and where to stand.

Selecting one opens the same lightbox as the studio gallery. Here, the caption reads eighty-five millimetres, shoot north from the lower steps.

## Permits

The permit table is the part that keeps photographers out of trouble. It lists portrait, wedding or engagement, commercial and video shoots, and whether each one needs a permit. Under it is the date the rules were verified, and a link to the official source.

When that date is more than six months old, a warning appears above the table. For Distillery District, the rules were last checked on March twentieth. The page tells the visitor to confirm with the official source before booking a shoot.

## Episode and rain backup

If there is an episode, it plays from the same kind of poster as on a studio review.

Then the rain backup. Outdoor shoots get rained out, so every spot lists the nearest reviewed studios, with the straight-line distance to each. For the arch bridge, Parkdale Daylight Loft is three point two kilometres away.

If no studios are published yet, the rain backup still appears. It says studio reviews are coming, and links to the studio list.

## The map

The map shows every reviewed studio and outdoor spot. Studio markers are dark squares with a camera. Spot markers are outlined circles with a tree. A legend sits in the corner.

Markers are real buttons, in the tab order. Selecting one opens a popup with the key fact and a link to the review. For Queen West Black Box, that is one hundred and twenty dollars all-in, with the stale price flag. Escape closes the popup and returns focus to the marker. A spot's popup shows its permit summary instead of a cost.

A switch above the map shows both kinds, studios only, or spots only. Here, only the five studios remain.

Below the map, every location is listed as a card, so nothing depends on the map alone.

## Map states

While locations load, the map says so. If they don't arrive, the page offers Try again, or a way to browse the lists instead. And before anything is published, the empty map explains why, and links to how we review.

On a phone, the map sits above the list. The MVP has no separate map and list toggle.

## Recap

Things to remember. Spot cards lead with the permit rule. A spot review gives best light by time and season, sample shots with focal lengths, and permits with a verified date and an official source. Stale permit rules are flagged. Every spot has a rain backup. And the map is never the only way in.
