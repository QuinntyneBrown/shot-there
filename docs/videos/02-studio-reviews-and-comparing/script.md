# 02 · Studio reviews and comparing

This walkthrough opens a studio review, then puts studios side by side. Every review has the same structure, so once you know one page, you know them all.

## The top of a review

Here is Parkdale Daylight Loft. The top of the page answers the first three questions anyone has: what does it cost, how good is it, and where is it. The all-in cost for a standard two-hour shoot is one hundred and fifty-five dollars, the overall score is three point eight out of five, and the address sits under the name.

On a wide screen, a summary panel stays beside the review as you scroll, with the price, the score, the review date and a button to book on the studio's own site. That link opens in a new tab.

On a phone, the same summary sits right under the photo.

## Pricing, specs and logistics

Pricing shows how the all-in cost is built. Two hours at sixty-five dollars, plus a twenty-five dollar cleaning fee. Mandatory fees are inside the all-in cost. Optional extras, like the strobe kit, are listed but kept out of it.

Measured specifications come next: usable floor space in metres and feet, ceiling height, window direction, power, cyc wall and backdrops. Natural light is rated for morning, midday, afternoon and evening, so you know when to book.

Then gear and logistics. What is included in the rate, what costs extra, and the practical things that ruin a shoot day: load-in, parking, transit, washroom, changing area, client seating and noise.

## Test shots and the gallery

This is the heart of ShotThere. Every studio gets the same three standard test shots: window light only, one strobe in the largest included modifier, and a full-length shot with two strobes. Same stand-in, same camera, same settings. That is what makes studios comparable.

Below them is a gallery from a real shoot. Selecting any image opens the lightbox.

The lightbox shows the image large, with its caption and its position, here image three of eight. Focus starts on Close and stays inside the lightbox. Escape closes it and returns focus to the thumbnail. Next on the last image wraps to the first. In the MVP, there is Next and Close, but no Previous.

If the full image takes longer than three hundred milliseconds, a spinner appears. The caption and controls stay usable while it loads.

## Verdict, disclosure and episode

The verdict gives the overall score, which is the mean of six scores: value, light, space, gear, logistics and condition. It says plainly who the studio is for, and who should skip it.

Under it is the disclosure. For this studio, we paid the full rate. If a studio gave us a discount or a free session, the review says so, and names who.

When there is an episode, the review links to it. The poster is a plain link to YouTube. With JavaScript, it becomes a button that loads the privacy-friendly player, without autoplay. Featured in lists the guides that recommend this studio.

## When a review is sparse

Not every review has everything. Junction Cyc Studio has no episode, so there is no episode section at all. It is in no published guide, so there is no Featured in. Nothing empty is left on the page.

And if one gallery image fails to load, its slot keeps the same size and shows the image's description instead. Nothing on the page shifts.

## Choosing studios to compare

Back on the studio list, each card has a Compare button. Select one, and a bar appears at the bottom of the screen. With one studio selected, its Compare button stays disabled, and the bar asks for at least two.

Select a second, and the bar offers to compare two studios.

The limit is four. Try to add a fifth and the bar says why it can't, and which studio to remove first. The selection is remembered in the browser, so it survives a reload.

## The comparison page

The comparison puts every chosen studio in its own column. Price comes first, with the lowest all-in cost marked, and stale prices flagged. Then space and light, gear and logistics, and all six scores, with the overall score in the last row.

At the bottom, the three standard test shots line up across the columns. Same setup, different rooms. This is the comparison the whole site is built for.

On a phone, the table scrolls sideways inside its own frame. The label column stays put, and each studio column stays readable.

## When a comparison link is wrong

Comparisons are shared as links, and links go wrong. If a link names five studios, the page shows the first four and says why.

If a studio in the link isn't published, the page names the missing one, and compares the rest.

And with fewer than two valid studios, the page explains how to pick studios, with a button back to the list.

## Recap

Things to remember. Every review follows the same order: cost, specs, logistics, test shots, verdict. The standard test shots are what make studios comparable. Up to four studios compare side by side, and a broken comparison link still shows what it can.
