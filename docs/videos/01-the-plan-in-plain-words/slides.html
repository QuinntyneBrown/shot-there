One founder, four hats

Photographerdoes every review
Architectbuilds the site
Salespersonsigns sponsors and licences
Communicatorhosts the channel

AI carries half of each hat

AI does

  • Builds the site from the written requirements
  • Drafts the scripts, captions and chapters
  • Drafts the search pages from the measurement sheet
  • Drafts the replies in the inbox

AI never

  • Visits a room
  • Measures a room
  • Scores a room
  • Writes the verdict

The founder is in every room, every time.

Four jars of money

Reading: always free · No jar buys a score

Jar 1 · The channel

Ads · sponsors, never a place under review · affiliate links on the test kit · members at $5 a month

Jar 2 · The camera

The review shoot is a paid client session · studio photos at one fixed price, after publication · workshops

Jar 3 · The maps

Illustrated shoot maps at $15 · the yearly Toronto studio report · location scouting for brands

Jar 4 · The computer

Other cities rent the method at $800 a month · data licensed to apps · a $19 spec sheet for a studio's own site

The biggest risk isn't money. It's trust.

A review site that gets paid by the places it reviews stops being a review site.

The founder never…

  • sells a score or a position
  • lets a studio see a review early, except to check the facts
  • takes a sponsor from a place under review
  • lets AI measure or score a room
  • lists a place that hasn't been shot there
  • hides a bad finding

Every number has a date

Older than six months? It gets a flag on the page.

The map stays honest, or it isn't worth anything.

Things to remember

  • Free to read, and nothing is listed unless it has been shot there.
  • One founder, four hats, AI carrying half of each. AI never measures, never scores.
  • Four jars: the channel, the camera, the maps and the computer.
  • The camera pays first. The other three pay most by year three.
  • Trust is the product. No jar buys a score.
