Score and pips

Every studio is scored on the same six-part rubric and every location's natural light is rated on the same four-step scale. Scores always show a number or a word, with pips beside it as a quick visual read, so locations compare side by side.

Overview

Use these parts wherever a review states a measured judgement:

  • Rubric scores sit in a studio's verdict. There are six categories, each a whole number from 1 to 5: Value, Light, Space, Gear, Logistics and Condition (L2-011). Each row shows the category, five pips and the number.
  • Overall score is the mean of the six scores rounded to one decimal place, calculated by the server and never by the page (L2-011.1). The Junction Loft scores 4 5 3 4 2 5, so it shows 3.8.
  • Light ratings describe natural light at each time of day on a four-step scale: None, Low, Medium or Strong, drawn as zero to three pips beside the word (L2-006.4). Studios show four times of day in the light grid; spots show five in a stacked table.

A score is always a number (or a word for light) plus pips. Never show it as pips alone or as colour alone (L2-068.1). Where space is tight, such as a location card, a guide entry or a comparison table cell, drop the pips and keep the number with "out of 5" for assistive technology. Pips are a reading aid. They are never a control: the owner enters scores as numbers in the studio editor.

Anatomy

  1. Overall value.score-overall__value: display type, tabular figures, always one decimal ("3.8", "4.0").
  2. Scale.score-overall__max: "overall, out of 5" in muted text. It belongs to the sentence a screen reader hears.
  3. Category label.score__label: Value, Light, Space, Gear, Logistics or Condition. Hidden from assistive technology because the row's hidden sentence repeats it.
  4. Pips.pips: five <i> bars, 20 × 8 px, 4 px apart. Filled bars carry data-on and are ink; empty bars are a 1 px edge in --color-border-strong.
  5. Number.score__value: right-aligned in a 2ch column so the pips line up down the list.
  6. Row.score: a 44 px tall grid row with a hairline divider underneath, plus a visually hidden "Light: 5 out of 5".

Light rating

  1. Time of day.light-grid__time: Morning, Midday, Afternoon, Evening (studios); spots add Golden hour and Blue hour.
  2. PipsThree narrower bars (16 × 8 px): None 0, Low 1, Medium 2, Strong 3. Hidden from assistive technology.
  3. Rating wordThe text of .light-rating: None, Low, Medium or Strong. This is what is read out.
  4. Tile.light-grid__item: sunken surface, 16 px padding, 12 px corners. An optional .light-grid__note line goes under the rating.

Sizes

There are two pip sizes. Both are 8 px tall, which is enough to tell filled from empty without competing with the number.

Rubric · five pips, 20 × 8 px (--space-5 × --space-2) Medium Light · three pips, 16 × 8 px (--space-4 × --space-2)
Pips never scale with the viewport. The overall value uses --text-display, the largest type on the verdict.

States

Pips are static: no pointer, focus, pressed or disabled states. The states are the values. A rubric score is 1 to 5, so a rubric row never has five empty pips. A light rating can be None, which shows three empty pips with the word.

Responsive behaviour

  • Score list: one column at XS and SM. From 768 px (48rem) it splits into two columns with a 48 px (--space-12) gap. Rows fill across, so Value and Light share the first line.
  • Score row: a three-column grid (label, pips, 2ch number). The label takes the leftover width. Pips are flex: none and never shrink. At 320 px "Condition" plus five pips plus the number still fits on one line.
  • Light grid: tiles stack at XS and SM and become one row of four from 768 px (L2-006.4).
  • Light table (spots): below 768 px the table becomes one card per time of day, with "Rating" and "Note" labels over the values (.table--stack).
  • Overall: the value keeps display size at every width. .score-overall lifts the paragraph measure so value and scale stay on one line.
  • Rows are 44 px tall (--target-comfortable) so the list keeps an even rhythm on phones. That height is for rhythm only: the rows are not targets.

Theming

Pips use two roles only: ink (--color-fg-default) for filled bars and the strong border (--color-border-strong) for the empty edge. They pick up the theme with no overrides. In the dark theme filled pips turn near-white and the empty edge stays mid-stone, so filled and empty still read apart on the dark canvas and on sunken tiles. Pips never use accent red or status colours, because a colour scale would turn a score into a judgement colour and fail people who cannot tell the hues apart.

/* Pips follow whatever ink and edge the surface defines */
.pips > i { box-shadow: inset 0 0 0 var(--border-width-hairline) var(--color-border-strong); }
.pips > i[data-on] { background: var(--color-fg-default); box-shadow: none; }

Forced colours: backgrounds and box shadows are removed in Windows High Contrast, so components.css opts the pips out (forced-color-adjust: none): each bar gets a 1px CanvasText border and filled bars a CanvasText fill. The number or word stays as well, so no meaning is lost.

Accessibility

Role and pattern

Plain text in native lists: <ul>/<li> for the score list and light grid, <p> for the overall, <table> for spot light. No ARIA widget role. The pips are presentational (aria-hidden="true"). They are not a meter or img, because the text already carries the value.

Keyboard

KeyAction
TabPasses over scores: nothing here is focusable.
Screen reader arrowsRead each row as one sentence, "Light: 5 out of 5", then the next row.

Focus

Nothing takes focus. Do not add tabindex to a score so that it can be "read". Screen readers reach it in browse mode.

Labelling

Each rubric row hides its visible label, pips and number (aria-hidden) and supplies one visually hidden sentence, "Value: 4 out of 5" (L2-011.3). The list is named "Rubric scores". The overall reads as "3.8 overall, out of 5". A light rating reads as its time and word, "Morning Medium". Compact scores add a hidden " out of 5" after the number.

Filled and empty pips are told apart by fill, not by hue. The empty edge meets 3:1 against every surface the pips sit on (L2-068.1). Colour is never the only cue (1.4.1): the number or word is always there.

Motion

None. Scores do not animate in or count up.

Touch

Not interactive, so there are no targets. Rows keep a 44 px rhythm and the 8 px pips stay legible at phone width. Pinch zoom is never blocked.

Content

  • Category names are fixed and in this order: Value, Light, Space, Gear, Logistics, Condition. Never abbreviate them on the page ("V", "Lo").
  • Rubric scores are whole numbers ("4"). The overall always has one decimal, even when it is round: "4.0", not "4".
  • The scale is "out of 5". Write "overall, out of 5" beside the overall and "/ 5" in comparison cells. Never use percentages or another scale.
  • Light words are None, Low, Medium and Strong, capitalised. Spot notes are one short sentence about direction or shade: "West light rakes the brick; the best hour here."
  • The overall comes from the server (overallScore). The page never re-averages, so a rounding difference cannot appear between the list, the comparison and the JSON-LD.

In Angular the pip count comes from a view model beside the page (for example pips(score, max) returning booleans). The template never computes the overall: it prints overallScore from the API, formatted to one decimal.
