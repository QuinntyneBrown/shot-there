Episode poster

Every review with a companion episode ends its long sections with "Watch the episode": a still from the shoot with a Play episode control. Nothing from YouTube loads until the visitor asks for it.

Overview

The episode poster is the video section of a studio or outdoor spot review (L2-047, L2-020). It has two lives:

  • Poster (on load): a link to the episode on YouTube, filled with a 16:9 still and a solid ink "Play episode" pill. No YouTube iframe, script or cookie is on the page (L2-047.1). Without JavaScript the link simply opens the video on YouTube (L2-047.3).
  • Player (after activation): the poster is replaced in the same 16:9 slot by a .video-frame iframe from youtube-nocookie.com, titled with the episode name and with no autoplay parameter (L2-047.2). Focus moves to the player.

Under either, .episode__links gives the episode title with its running time and a "Watch on YouTube" link (L2-048.2).

Use it only for a ShotThere episode about the location on the page. When a review has no video ID, render no section at all: no heading, no empty poster, no "coming soon" (L2-020.1, see pages/spot/no-video and pages/studio/minimal). Photos of the location belong in the shot grid, not here.

Anatomy

  1. ContainerThe <a> itself: full width, aspect-ratio: 16 / 9, --radius-lg, overflow: hidden, ground --color-bg-inverse so the slot never flashes white while the still loads.
  2. Poster stillA frame from the episode, object-fit: cover, alt="" because the play label names the link. Lazy-loaded; it sits below the fold on every review.
  3. Play pill.video-poster__play: centred, 52px tall (--control-height-lg), ink fill --color-action, --shadow-3 so it reads on any still.
  4. Play glyph and labelFilled triangle (aria-hidden) and "Play episode", plus a visually hidden ": Episode 4, The Junction Loft" so the link's name is unique on the page.
  5. Episode links.episode__links: the full episode title and running time in .text-muted, then the external "Watch on YouTube" link. Wraps onto two lines on phones.

Poster and player

Sizes

One size: fluid. The poster and the player fill the review's main column at aspect-ratio: 16 / 9 (L2-020.2) at every width, so the slot height is known before the still or the player arrives and nothing shifts. The play pill is fixed at 52px tall (--control-height-lg) with --space-6 side padding, --font-size-md label and a 24px glyph, and does not scale with the poster.

Responsive behaviour

  • The poster and player are always the full width of the review's main column (.review-main) and always 16:9. From LG the column sits beside the summary panel; below LG it is the page width minus the layout margin.
  • The play pill never wraps (white-space: nowrap); "Play episode" with its glyph is about 170px wide, which fits the 288px poster on a 320px phone.
  • .episode__links is a wrapping row: when the column is narrow the long episode title takes its own line and "Watch on YouTube" drops beneath it, 4px apart; when both fit they share a line, 24px apart.
  • The player keeps the page's horizontal rhythm: no full-bleed video on phones, so the review never scrolls sideways.

Theming

The poster reads semantic tokens directly: --color-bg-inverse for the slot ground, --color-action / --color-action-hover / --color-action-active / --color-fg-on-action for the pill (it is the same ink as a primary button), --color-fg-inverse for the failed-poster text and --shadow-3 for the pill's lift.

In dark the inverse ground turns light stone (--palette-stone-50) and the pill flips to light stone with near-black text, so the failed poster becomes a pale slot with dark text and the pill still stands out from it by its shadow and fill. The still itself is content and keeps its own colours in both themes. YouTube's player is outside our theming; the inverse ground only shows while it loads.

To re-skin the pill without touching .video-poster__play, override the action tokens on a wrapper: .review-section { --color-action: …; --color-fg-on-action: …; }.

Accessibility

Role and pattern

The poster is a native link (<a href="https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=…">, APG Link), which is what makes it work without JavaScript (L2-047.3). With JavaScript the click is intercepted: navigation is prevented and the iframe is inserted. The player is a native <iframe> whose title names the episode (L2-047.2). The section is a <section aria-labelledby> under the heading "Watch the episode".

Keyboard

KeyAction
Tab / Shift+TabMoves to the poster, then to "Watch on YouTube".
EnterOn the poster: swaps in the player and moves focus to it. Without JavaScript: opens the episode on YouTube.
Inside the playerYouTube's own keys (Space or K to play, arrows to seek). Tab leaves the iframe to "Watch on YouTube"; there is no trap.

Focus

The shared ring (2px --color-focus-ring at --focus-ring-offset) draws around the whole poster, following its --radius-lg corners. After activation focus moves to the iframe so a keyboard or screen-reader user lands in the player, not on <body> where the poster used to be.

Labelling

The link's name is "Play episode: Episode 4, The Junction Loft": the visible label plus a visually hidden episode name, so it is unique and starts with the visible words (2.5.3 label in name). The still has alt=""; the failed-poster fallback is aria-hidden="true" because it repeats the name. The iframe title is the full episode title. "Watch on YouTube" carries a hidden "(opens in a new tab)".

The pill sits on a photo, which no token can guarantee; its solid fill carries the label's contrast and --shadow-3 separates it from light and dark stills alike. On a failed poster the pill and the ground are the same ink, so only the label and the shadow show; the label keeps its 4.5:1 and the whole poster is the target, so nothing depends on seeing the pill's edge.

Motion

Nothing moves: the hover fill changes without a transition, and the player never autoplays (L2-047.2). Reduced motion needs no extra rule.

Touch

The whole 16:9 poster is the target, far above 44 × 44 even on a 320px phone; the 52px pill marks where to tap. Hover only darkens the pill, so touch loses nothing.

Content

  • Section heading: "Watch the episode". Control: "Play episode", exactly (L2-047.1). Never "Watch now", "Click to play" or a bare "Play".
  • Hidden suffix: ": Episode 4, The Junction Loft" — episode number and location only.
  • Episode line: the YouTube title as published, then a middle dot and the running time: "Episode 7: Evergreen Brick Works at golden hour · 12:48".
  • External link: "Watch on YouTube" plus hidden "(opens in a new tab)" (L2-048.2).
  • iframe title: the full episode title, "Episode 4: The Junction Loft — big north light, tiny elevator".
  • Failed poster: the short title only, "Episode 4: The Junction Loft"; no error wording, because nothing is broken for the visitor.

Drift fixed during extraction:

  • The studio mocks point the poster at playing.html so the mock set can be clicked through, while the spot mock points at the YouTube watch URL. The system's poster href is always the watch URL (L2-047.3).
  • Poster stills are 1200 × 800 (3:2) in the mocks and get cropped into the 16:9 slot. Supply stills at 16:9 (for example 1280 × 720) so nothing important is cut.
  • On pages/studio/media-failed at 320px and 360px the centred pill sat on top of the fallback title (title 7072–7093px, pill 7086–7138px at 360px). Fixed: .video-poster:has(> .media-fallback) .video-poster__play moves the pill to the bottom of a failed poster. The title was always in the link's hidden name, so nothing was lost for assistive technology.
  • .video-poster had no pressed style; the pages show active as the hover fill. Fixed: .video-poster:active and [data-state="active"] give the pill --color-action-active.
