Pattern

Content and tone

ShotThere writes like someone who has been in the room with a light meter: first-hand, specific and plain, as happy to report a tiny freight elevator as big north light. Visitors get facts they can compare; the admin gets short instructions and honest consequences.

Overview

The voice follows from the promise: nothing is listed unless it has been shot there. So the copy:

  • Reports, doesn't sell. Measurements, prices and what happened on the shoot: "Noise: streetcar rumble every few minutes." No "stunning", "perfect" or "must-visit".
  • Publishes the bad with the good. "Who should skip it: video crews, because of the streetcar, and anyone hauling heavy cases: the freight elevator is tiny." A low score stays up.
  • Says how it knows. Every price and permit rule carries its verified date; every review says how the session was paid for; the test setup is spelled out.
  • Speaks as "we", to "you". The disclosure statements are the one first-person-singular exception, fixed word for word by the editorial policy ("I paid the full rate for this session.").
  • Canadian English, sentence case: colour, centre, neighbourhood; "Measured specifications", not "Measured Specifications".

The general rules (numbers, dates, the error and empty-state formulas, the microcopy library) are on Content. This page applies them to ShotThere's flows.

The all-in sentence explains its own arithmetic. Permit rules always name the official source and the date; when stale, the warning says how old and who to confirm with.Sources: pages/studio/default, pages/spot/stale-permit
  • All-in is "the cost of a 2-hour shoot with mandatory fees": rate × the greater of 2 hours and the minimum, plus every mandatory fee. Optional fees say "Optional, not in all-in".
  • Permit rules are information, not advice. ShotThere reports what the official source said on the verified date and sends people to it; it never says a shoot "is allowed". Write "Not required" or "Required" per shoot type, and "Confirm with … before you shoot" when stale.

Responsive behaviour

  • Copy is the same at every width; nothing is truncated with an ellipsis except the compare bar's list of names, which is also in the cards.
  • Tight surfaces use the agreed short forms, not new wording: "All-in 2 h" on cards, "All-in, 2 hours" in the summary panel and comparison, "All-in for 2 hours" in sentences.
  • Long studio names and addresses wrap ("Leslieville Daylight Studio", "Dundas St W at Pacific Ave, 2nd floor"); measurements keep metric and imperial together on one line where they fit.

Accessibility

Role and pattern

Plain language at a general reading level; one idea per sentence in errors and help. Headings describe their section ("Standard test shots", "Rain backup"), so a heading list is a usable table of contents.

Keyboard

Not affected by copy, except that link and button text must make sense out of context, because keyboard and screen-reader users often reach them through a list of links.

Focus

Messages that receive focus (the form summary) start with their heading, so the first thing read is the count and the goal.

Labelling

  • Links say where they go: "Read the full review of Kensington Window Room", never "Read more" or "Click here". Repeated links get a hidden suffix.
  • Alt text on photos that carry information describes the light and the result, starting with the shot: "Test shot 2, full length on the cyc: the scuffs show as faint marks behind the feet." Card thumbnails repeat the name beside them, so they are alt="".
  • Abbreviations are expanded once or avoided: "2 h" only beside "All-in"; "m" and "ft" always with a number.

Content

  • Money: CAD with cents and no currency code: "$45.50", "$145.00".
  • Dates: "3 Oct 2026" in lists, tables and admin; "29 September 2026" in a review's head and verified lines; "Monday 13 October 2026" only when the weekday matters. Times: "4:12 pm", "10:02 am".
  • Measurements: metric first, imperial in brackets: "6.1 m × 4.3 m (20 ft × 14 ft)", "4.0 m (13 ft)", with the multiplication sign.
  • Ranges: "from 1 to 5", "153 to 183 days old"; ages in days: "225 days ago".
  • Quotes: curly quotes around searched text: No studios match “parkdale”.
  • Missing values: "—" in tables; words where the review states them: "No", "None", "Nothing costs extra".

Drift fixed during extraction: the compare toggle reads "Compare" / "Remove" on cards and "Add to compare" / "Remove from compare" on the review; both are kept, the short form for cards and the long one where there is room, and the bar always says Compare. Counts mix "2 draft" and "2 drafts"; the system writes "7 published, 2 draft" (status words, not plurals). Alert titles mix full stops; titles take none. The spot card's "Permit info may be out of date" becomes "Permit rules may be out of date", matching the verified line. No mock shows a standing permit disclaimer while the rules are fresh (the README only notes that permit data is illustrative); the verified date, the official source link and "Confirm with … before you shoot" carry it, and a standing line is an open question for the product owner. The spot light table's "Rating" column header describes the light level, not a review rating; consider "Light" to avoid confusion with scores.
