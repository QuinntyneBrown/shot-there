Foundation

Color

A white canvas, near-black ink and one accent red that only ever means "selected". The photos of each studio and spot bring the colour; the interface stays out of their way.

Overview

ShotThere is a review site for places to shoot, so the most important colour on any screen is in the photographs: the north light in The Junction Loft, the brick at Evergreen Brick Works. The interface is almost entirely the white (in dark, stone-950) canvas and warm near-black ink, so photos, test shots and measurements read cleanly side by side.

Five hues, each with one job. Token names keep their palette names for stability; read them as labels, not meanings:

  • Neutral stone (--palette-stone-*): canvas, ink, text, borders, photo slots and the "info" status.
  • Accent red (--palette-pepper-*): selected and current state, focus, hovered links, the open map marker.
  • Success green (--palette-callaloo-*): "Published", "Lowest", "Not required", saved.
  • Warning amber (--palette-mango-*): "Prices may be out of date", "Stale", "Too many sign-in attempts".
  • Danger red (--palette-sorrel-*): field errors, failed uploads, "Delete studio".

Colour has two tiers. Primitives (--palette-*) are raw values that no component reads. Semantic roles (--color-*) say what a colour is for and are the only tier a theme overrides. Components alias roles through their own tokens (--btn-bg, --alert-fg); see Theming.

Five rules carry the look:

  1. Ink is action. The one primary button per screen ("Book on the studio's site", "Compare" in the compare bar) is solid --color-action, like the wordmark.
  2. Accent red is "selected". The pressed compare toggle, the selected segment, checked boxes, the focus ring and the open map marker. Never a button fill, never decoration.
  3. Info is stone, not blue. The one hue already means something, so neutral information stays grey.
  4. Danger red is deeper and cooler than the accent, so an error never reads as a selection.
  5. Shape before colour. Studios and spots on the map differ by shape (ink square, ringed circle); a score is filled pips plus its number. Colour is never the only signal (WCAG 1.4.1).

Primitive ramps

Each ramp runs lightest to darkest. Steps exist only where a semantic role needs them, so the ramps have gaps by design: stone has 15 steps because text, borders and dark surfaces need fine control; the status hues have six to eight. Hover a step to see its value; every step with its light and dark swatch is in Tokens.

Neutral stone

Warm greys, so the interface never looks blue-grey beside warm studio photos. Light text sits at 950 (ink), 700 (muted) and 600 (subtle); dark text at 100, 350 and 400. Dark surfaces climb from 950 (canvas) through 900 (sunken) and 850 (raised) to 800 (subtle). Stone-500 is the 3:1 control edge in both themes, and stone-100 / stone-800 fill an empty photo slot.

Accent red

Light theme uses 600 for selection, focus and hovered links (5.9:1 on white), with 50 and 100 as the pressed compare toggle's fill. Dark theme flips to 300 on stone-950 (8.3:1) with 950 and 900 as fills. 400 and 500 are spare.

Success green, warning amber and danger red

Each status hue has a tinted fill (50 light, 950 dark), a readable text step (700 or 600 light, 300 dark), a border step and an icon step. Danger red also has 100 for the dark destructive-button hover. The 900 steps are spare.

Semantic roles

Components read these and nothing else. The swatches show the value for the theme you are viewing; press t or use the toggle in the sidebar to switch. Light and dark values side by side are in Tokens.

Backgrounds

Surfaces are flat. Canvas, surface and raised are all white in light theme: white space and the odd hairline separate a review's sections, not tinted panels. Only sunken (the all-in price block, light-grid cells, the disclosure, fee rows) and subtle (hover, skeletons) are tinted. --color-bg-photo is its own role so an unloaded photo slot can differ from a sunken panel.

Photos, map markers and pips

Photo slots

Every image slot (location card media, review hero, test and gallery shots, editor thumbnails) is filled with --color-bg-photo until the image arrives, so the layout never jumps. If an image fails, the slot keeps its size and shows the alt text (L2-086.1).

The Junction Loft: two tall north windows over a brick wall.
Loaded · loading on --color-bg-photo · failed, showing alt text (pages/studio/media-failed).

Photos are content: they keep their own colours in both themes. Never tint a photo, frame it in a brand colour or lay a coloured overlay on it.

Map markers: shape carries the type

A studio is an ink square (--color-action, --radius-xs); a spot is a ringed circle (--color-bg-surface fill inside a 4px --color-fg-default ring, --radius-full). Both sit on a 2px --color-bg-surface halo with --shadow-2, so they read on any map tile, and the legend repeats the shapes (L2-027.1). The marker whose popup is open adds an accent ring (aria-expanded="true").

The ink square is checked at 18.6:1 against the canvas (pair 55) and the spot ring is default ink; map tiles are content, so the surface halo, not the tile, is the adjacent colour that guarantees 3:1. In dark theme the square turns light (stone-100) and the ring follows the text colour, so the two shapes still differ by fill: solid versus hollow.

Pips: ink versus a 3:1 edge

Rubric scores (five pips) and natural-light levels (three pips) use filled pips in --color-fg-default and empty pips drawn as a hairline --color-border-strong ring, which holds 3:1 on the surface and on the sunken light-grid cell (L2-068.1). The number or word always sits beside the pips, so the value is in text too (L2-068.3).

Four tones, each with the same five parts: a tinted bg, readable fg, decorative border, 3:1 icon and a solid for filled marks. Status colour never stands alone: alerts carry an icon and a title, badges a word and a dot, status labels a word and a filled or hollow dot.

Why info is stone

ShotThere has one hue with a meaning, so a blue would be a second brand colour competing with the photos, and an accent-red info banner would look like a selection. Neutral announcements ("Your session expired", "One studio in this link couldn't be found") are stone-50 with stone-800 text: calm, legible and clearly not an error.

Accent and danger side by side

Both are reds, so they are kept apart on two axes. Danger red is darker (danger-600 is 7.4:1 on white against the accent's 5.9:1) and cooler (it leans toward magenta, the accent toward orange). Errors also always arrive with the danger icon and words; selection arrives with a changed label, semibold text or a filled box.

--color-accentSelected edges: the pressed compare toggle, checked boxes, the selected button-group segment, the open map marker's ring.
--color-danger-solidDestructive button fill ("Delete"), invalid input edge, failed progress fill, count badge.

Light and dark

Dark theme is not an inversion. It keeps the same hierarchy with different steps: the canvas becomes stone-950, text stone-100, raised surfaces (the compare bar, dialogs, the map popup) lift to stone-850 because shadows hardly read on near-black, the accent and the status hues jump to their 300 steps, and every solid fill takes dark text. Both themes use the same component CSS; only the --color-* tier changes.

Usage

  • Read roles, never primitives or hex. Write color: var(--color-fg-muted), never var(--palette-stone-700) and never a hex. The design-system checker fails any raw colour in component CSS and in the docs.
  • One ink fill per screen. The primary button is the only large ink surface; the studio marker, the play pill and progress fills are small ink marks.
  • Accent only for state. Pressed, selected, current, focused, hovered link, open marker. If removing the accent would not change what the visitor thinks is selected, it should not be accent.
  • Tint before solid. Status meaning uses the tinted -bg with -fg text. Solids are for small marks and the destructive button inside a confirmation dialog.
  • Muted for the supporting line. Every location card is name in ink, "neighbourhood · reviewed date" in muted, facts in ink. Do not add a third colour.
  • Subtle is the floor for text. Subtle text is checked on canvas, surface, raised, sunken, subtle and photo only.
  • Shape carries type, colour carries state. Studio versus spot is square versus ring; open versus closed is the accent ring.
  • Photos own the colour. Do not tint photos, test shots or map tiles, and do not put coloured section backgrounds behind a shot grid.

Adding a brand theme

A theme overrides the semantic tier only. To add, for example, a high-contrast theme:

  1. Add any new primitives to tokens/tokens.css as --palette-* steps (never inline hexes in the theme block).
  2. Add a block [data-theme="high-contrast"] { … } that reassigns every --color-* role in the dark block's list, plus color-scheme.
  3. Keep the meanings: one action colour, one "selected" colour that is never a button fill, info neutral, danger distinct from the selected hue, studio and spot markers still different in shape.
  4. Run scripts/check_contrast.py for the new theme; every pair in contrast-pairs.json must pass. Add a pair whenever you introduce a new foreground/background combination.
  5. Regenerate tokens.json and check the theme toggle on this page.
[data-theme="high-contrast"] {
  --color-fg-muted: var(--palette-stone-950);
  --color-fg-subtle: var(--palette-stone-800);
  --color-border-default: var(--palette-stone-700);
  --color-accent: var(--palette-pepper-800);
  /* …every other --color-* role… */
  color-scheme: light;
}

Accessibility

ShotThere meets WCAG 2.2 AA in both themes: 4.5:1 for text (1.4.3), 3:1 for large text, control edges, focus indicators, meaningful icons, map markers and pips (1.4.11, L2-068.1), and never colour alone (1.4.1). Every foreground/background combination a component uses is listed in tokens/contrast-pairs.json and checked in both themes by scripts/check_contrast.py. The tables below recompute each ratio live in your browser.

What the checks cover

  • Subtle text on sunken panels. Light-grid times, fee-row labels and "All-in, 2 hours" in the price block are --color-fg-subtle on --color-bg-surface-sunken: stone-600 holds 5.1:1 there (pairs 64 and 104).
  • Empty pips are a 3:1 edge, not a pale fill, so a score of 2 out of 5 is still readable as three empty slots (pair 99).
  • Photo slots. Alt-text fallbacks and the loading spinner are checked on --color-bg-photo (pairs 87, 97 and 98), because that is what a visitor sees on a slow connection.
  • Hovered admin rows. "Published", "Stale" and "Due soon" are checked on --color-bg-subtle as well as the surface (pairs 88–91).
  • Focus on tints. The focus ring is checked on all four alert tints, so a "Try again" button inside an alert keeps a visible ring (pairs 80–83).

Preferences

With prefers-contrast: more, default borders become strong and muted and subtle text step up a level. With forced-colors: active, the focus ring becomes Highlight and borders CanvasText, so every control keeps a visible edge; the markers keep their shapes because shape, not colour, carries the type.

Some pairs appear twice on purpose: the same combination is checked once for each place it is used.
