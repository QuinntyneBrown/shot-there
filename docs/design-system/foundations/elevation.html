Foundation

Elevation

ShotThere is flat. Location cards sit straight on the canvas and review sections are separated by space; shadows exist only for what floats above the page: the compare bar, map markers and their popup, toasts, dialogs and sheets.

Overview

The photos are the content, so nothing on the page competes with them for depth. Page content is flat at every viewport, and depth appears only when something genuinely sits above the page and must be told apart from it.

  • On the page: no shadow. Location cards have neither border nor shadow; the summary panel, stat tiles, editor sections and the editor aside have a 1px --color-border-default hairline at most, even where they stick.
  • On hover: a level-2 lift on the primary button and interactive cards, as feedback, not structure.
  • On the map: markers and the legend overlay use level 2 so they read on any tile.
  • Floating: the compare bar, the map popup, popovers, combobox suggestions, toasts and the episode play pill use level 3.
  • Modal: dialogs, side sheets and drawers use level 4 over a backdrop. The photo lightbox covers the whole screen with the canvas colour, so it needs no shadow.

Elevation has two parts: shadows (--shadow-*) say how far a surface floats, and z-order (--z-*) decides what covers what. They are separate scales because a toast (level 3) must still sit above a dialog (level 4), and the compare bar (level 3) must sit below one.

Shadow levels

Four levels, each softer and wider than a typical material shadow so they read as lift rather than outline. All are tinted with --shadow-color, a warm stone (28 24 20) in light theme so shadows never look blue-grey beside warm photos.

Elevation in dark theme

A shadow is a darker area around a surface; on a near-black canvas there is almost nothing darker to show. Dark theme therefore does two things at once:

  1. Raised surfaces get lighter. The compare bar, the map popup, dialogs and sheets use stone-850 against the stone-950 canvas, so their edge is visible without any shadow.
  2. Shadows turn black and stronger. --shadow-color becomes pure black (0 0 0) and opacities rise from 6–18% to 35–65%, enough to darken the area around a panel.

The backdrop also darkens from ink at 50% to black at 66%, so a dialog never floats over a page that looks the same as itself. The focus shadow and its offset colour are restated in the dark blocks, so a region marked data-theme="dark" inside a light page resolves them correctly.

Z-order

Nine named layers with wide gaps between them. Components read the token; nobody writes z-index: 9999. A layer may use calc(var(--z-…) + 1) for a child that must sit above its own parent, never to jump to another layer.

TokenValueWhat sits there
--z-basePage content, map tiles and markers.
--z-raisedThe compare toggle and tags on a location card, above the card's stretched link; the compare table's sticky label column; a selected button-group segment.
--z-stickyThe compare bar. It stays above page content but under every overlay, so a dialog or the filters sheet covers it.
--z-dropdownReserved for a list floating over the page; nothing uses it today.
--z-overlayThe backdrop behind a dialog.
--z-modalDialogs, the filters and site-menu sheets, drawers and the photo lightbox.
--z-popoverPopovers and the map marker popup, which must clear the markers and legend around it.
--z-toastThe toast region: "You can compare up to 4 studios" stays visible over the compare bar, and an admin toast over the dialog that caused it.
--z-tooltipThe focused skip link: always on top.

It goes back to draft. Its page, its map marker and its place in lists, comparisons and guides disappear within a minute.

In the frames the bar, toast, popup and dialog are pinned to the frame instead of the window; in the product they are fixed to the viewport.

Usage

  • No shadow on the page. If something is in the page flow, including a sticky summary panel or editor aside, it does not get a shadow. Use space first, a hairline second.
  • Shadow follows the layer. Floating surfaces use level 3, modal panels level 4. Do not give the map popup level 4 to make it "pop".
  • Raised surfaces read the raised role. Every floating surface uses --color-bg-surface-raised, never --color-bg-surface, so dark theme can lift it.
  • Hover lift is feedback. Level 2 appears on hover only, and only on things that are clickable as a whole. Location cards scale their photo instead.
  • One modal at a time. Do not stack a dialog on a dialog; replace the content or move to a screen. Toasts may sit above a dialog; that is what their layer is for.
  • Toasts clear the compare bar. On screens with the bar, the toast region sits above the bar's height so the two never overlap.
  • Use the z tokens. No raw z-index numbers in component CSS.

Accessibility

  • Shadows are never the only edge. Dialog panels sit on a backdrop; the compare bar has a hairline top edge; popovers and the map popup have a 1px border; markers have a surface halo. In forced-colours mode shadows disappear and those edges become CanvasText, and dialogs and sheets add an outline.
  • Focus not obscured (2.4.11). The compare bar (100) never hides the focused element: pages that show it reserve --space-24 plus the safe-area inset at the bottom (L2-030.6). A focused control inside a dialog is never under the sticky dialog footer, because the footer is part of the scroll container.
  • Modal means inert. Content under the backdrop is inert and hidden from assistive technology while a dialog, sheet or the lightbox is open; z-order alone does not make something modal. Focus is trapped and returns to the invoking control on close (L2-067.3).
  • The map popup is not modal. It is a non-modal dialog: Escape closes it and returns focus to its marker (L2-028.2), and the rest of the map stays operable.
  • Toasts do not steal focus. They announce through a live region that exists before the first toast, even when they sit above a dialog.
  • Hover lift moves 1px and is a transition that respects prefers-reduced-motion through the motion tokens.
