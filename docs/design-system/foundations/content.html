Overview

The promise is on the home page: "Toronto photo studios and outdoor spots, reviewed first-hand. Nothing is listed unless it has been shot there. Same measurements, same test setup, side by side." The copy keeps that promise. A studio card is a name, a neighbourhood, a review date, an all-in cost and a score; a button is a verb; an error says what is safe and what to do. Every string lives in resource files and is marked for translation (L2-070.2), so these rules apply to the files, not just the screens.

  • First-hand. Say what happened in the room: "Streetcar rumble every few minutes; fine for stills, plan around it for video."
  • Measured. Numbers with units, prices with dates, scores out of 5. "6.1 m × 4.3 m (20 ft × 14 ft)", not "spacious".
  • Plain. Short sentences, common words, no marketing. No exclamation marks, no "Oops", no "stunning".
  • Honest about trade-offs. Every review has both "Who it's for" and "Who should skip it" (L2-011.3), and the bad stays in: "Scuffed cycs, tiny elevators, streetcar rumble and stale prices go in the review."

Voice and tone

The voice stays the same everywhere. The tone shifts with the moment.

MomentToneFrom the mocks
Browsing a listOut of the way. Names, places, costs and scores; one line of context."Every studio here was shot in with the same test setup. All-in is the cost of a 2-hour shoot with mandatory fees." · "7 studios"
Reading a reviewFirst-hand and specific. The good and the bad in the same flat voice."Who it's for: Portrait and fashion shooters who want big north light and a usable cyc on a mid-range budget." · "Who should skip it: Video crews, because of the streetcar, and anyone hauling heavy cases: the freight elevator is tiny."

Something went wrongCalm. Say what is safe, then what to do."The studio list didn't arrive. Check your connection and try again; your filters are kept."
Admin editingPractical and exact about consequences."It goes back to draft. Its page, its map marker and its place in lists, comparisons and guides disappear within a minute. You can publish it again any time."
DoneQuietly confirmed. A name and what happens next, no exclamation marks."Corktown Garage Studio is published. It appears in lists, the map and the sitemap within a minute." · "Link copied"
  • "I" is the reviewer, in first-hand statements: the disclosure ("I paid the full rate for this session.") and the editorial policy ("Nothing is listed unless I've booked it or visited it and shot there myself.").
  • "We" is ShotThere, in system copy and editors' notes: "We couldn't load the studios", "the nearest studios we've reviewed".
  • "You" is the reader: "Confirm with the studio before you book."
  • Use contractions: "can't", "we've", "it's", "didn't". They read as speech. Apostrophes are the plain ' throughout, as in every mock; quoted search terms use curly quotes: No studios match “parkdale”.
  • Use the en dash for ranges (153–183 days), the em dash with spaces in episode titles, the middle dot (·) between facts, and the ellipsis character (…) only for something in progress ("Saving…", "Loading studios…").
  • One idea per sentence. Most sentences in the product are under 20 words.

Money and the all-in cost

Prices are Canadian dollars with a dollar sign and two decimals, always: $60.00, $45.50, $145.00 (L2-004.4). No "CAD" label and no cents dropped from stored values. Round thresholds in titles and filters may drop the cents: "Best studios under $120 for 2 hours".

The all-in cost is the number visitors compare. It is the cost of a standard 2-hour shoot: the hourly rate times the larger of 2 hours or the minimum booking, plus every mandatory fee. Optional fees are listed and excluded. The server calculates it (L2-004.5); copy never recalculates it.

all-in = rate × max(2, minimum hours) + mandatory fees
The Junction Loft: $60.00 × 2 + $25.00 cleaning = $145.00
Ossington Blackbox: $90.00 × 3 = $270.00

Scores and light

Six rubric scores, each a whole number from 1 to 5: Value, Light, Space, Gear, Logistics, Condition, always in that order. The overall is their mean to one decimal, calculated by the server: 4, 5, 3, 4, 2, 5 gives 3.8.

WhereShownRead by a screen reader
Category in the verdictLight · pips · 5Light: 5 out of 5
Overall in the verdict3.8 overall, out of 53.8 overall, out of 5
Card and summary panelScore 3.8 · Overall 3.83.8 out of 5
Comparison table, where the scale isn't stated nearby3.8 / 53.8 out of 5 (the " / 5" is hidden and replaced)

Always one decimal for the overall, even when it is whole: 4.0, 3.0. Never percentages and never an icon scale in place of the number. Natural light uses words, not scores: None, Low, Medium or Strong, each beside three pips, for morning, midday, afternoon and evening (spots add golden hour and blue hour).

  • Neighbourhoods are the names Torontonians use: Junction, Leslieville, Ossington, Liberty Village, Kensington Market, Bloordale, Riverside, Don Valley, Garden District.
  • Cross streets: "Queen St E at Carlaw Ave", "Augusta Ave at Baldwin St". Add the floor or entrance only when it helps someone find the door: "2nd floor", "rear lane".

Usage

Accessibility

  • Plain language. Short sentences and common words help everyone, including people reading in a second language and people with cognitive disabilities. Explain jargon once where a visitor might not know it: "cyc wall" is fine for photographers; "20 A circuits" sits under Power.
  • Alt text describes what a photographer would look for: the light, the surface, the space. "Test shot 3, product on a table: clean highlight on the bottle, neutral background." Never "photo of", never the file name, never empty on an informative image (L2-057.4, L2-068.2).
  • Numbers read well aloud. Scores have their scale ("out of 5"); the " / 5" and "×" forms are for the eye, and the hidden text says it in words where needed.
  • Link and button text stands alone (2.4.4): "Read the full review of Kensington Window Room". Avoid "click", "tap" and "see"; say "open" or "choose" if a verb is needed at all.
  • Don't rely on shape, place or colour in instructions: not "the red badge" or "the square markers on the right". Say "studios" and "spots".
  • New tabs are announced with a hidden "(opens in a new tab)" after the link text.

Tokens

Content has no tokens of its own. The type roles that carry it (--text-body, --text-label, --text-caption, --text-overline, --text-prose) and the reading measure (--layout-measure, 62 characters) are documented on Typography and Layout.
