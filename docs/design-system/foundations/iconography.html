Iconography

Icons are small, plain outlines that support a word and rarely replace one. They are drawn on a 24-unit grid with a 2-unit round stroke in the colour of the text beside them. Photos of the studios and spots carry the pages; icons only help people act.

Overview

ShotThere uses few icons. They appear where they speed up a familiar action (close, menu, filters, search, previous and next photo, copy link, play episode), mark the tone of a message next to its words (success, info, warning, danger), point out a link that leaves the site, or stand in for a photo or a map that did not load. Every icon is an inline <svg class="icon"> copied from the icon sheet; there is no icon font and no sprite to load.

  • Outline only. Open shapes, no fills. The one exception is the play triangle on the episode poster, which is filled so it reads as "play" at a glance on any frame of video.
  • Text first. Most icons sit beside a visible label ("Filters", "Menu", "Copy link", "Play episode", "Try again"). Icon-only controls are limited to Close, Dismiss, Previous photo and Next photo.
  • Inherit colour. Icons draw with currentColor, so they are ink in ink text, muted in a muted caption and inverse on a toast, in both themes, with no extra tokens.
  • Scores and light are not icons. Rubric scores and natural-light levels use pips beside a number or a word; map markers are shapes on the map, explained by a legend. Neither belongs on this sheet.

Grid and stroke

  1. 24-unit canvasviewBox="0 0 24 24", with about 2 units of padding so strokes never touch the edge. Shapes sit on whole or half units.
  2. 2-unit strokestroke-width: var(--icon-stroke) (2), round caps and joins, fill: none. The stroke scales with the icon: 2px at 24px, 1.67px at the default 20px.
  3. Colour from textstroke: currentColor. Set color on the parent, never a stroke colour on the icon.

All of this comes from one rule in components.css, so an icon's markup is only its paths:

.icon { width: var(--icon-size-md); height: var(--icon-size-md); fill: none;
  stroke: currentColor; stroke-width: var(--icon-stroke);
  stroke-linecap: round; stroke-linejoin: round; flex: none; }

Drawing a new icon

  • Start from a 24 × 24 artboard with a 2-unit keyline; use 2-unit strokes, round caps and joins, and no fills.
  • Match the weight of the sheet: simple geometric shapes, at most three or four strokes, corners rounded by the stroke rather than drawn.
  • Draw dots (the point of "!" or "i") as h.01, a tiny segment that the round cap turns into a dot in every renderer.
  • Export paths only: no style, stroke or class attributes inside the SVG, no width/height, no <title>. The play triangle is the only path that sets fill="currentColor" stroke="none".
  • Add it to this sheet with its name and use before anyone ships it. One glyph means one thing everywhere (WCAG 3.2.4).

Sizes

Twenty pixels is the default: it sits on the cap height of 16px body text and fills a 44px button comfortably. The other sizes belong to specific places; pick by context, not by taste.

Colour

Icons take the colour of their context. The few places that colour an icon on purpose do it through the parent's color, using status tokens, and always next to words that say the same thing.

  • Status icons use --color-<status>-icon on their status background and keep 3:1 (WCAG 1.4.11). On the inverse toast surface they switch to the toast's text colour so every tone passes in both themes; the shape carries the tone.
  • The accent red reaches an icon only through a selected or current parent. Never colour an icon accent red to decorate it.
  • Disabled icons inherit the disabled text colour from their control.

Labelling

An icon is either decoration beside words, or the only visible label of a control. The markup differs.

The sliders and copy icons are decorative; the three icon-only buttons are named by aria-label.
HTML
<button class="btn" type="button">
  <svg class="icon" aria-hidden="true" viewBox="0 0 24 24">…</svg>Copy link
</button>
<button class="icon-btn" type="button" aria-label="Next photo">
  <svg class="icon" aria-hidden="true" viewBox="0 0 24 24"><path d="m9 6 6 6-6 6"/></svg>
</button>
  • Every SVG icon carries aria-hidden="true", including the one inside an icon-only button. The name belongs to the control, not the drawing, and no <title> goes inside the SVG.
  • Icon-only controls carry aria-label with a verb or the thing they move to: "Close", "Dismiss", "Previous photo", "Next photo". Inside a dialog, "Close" is enough because the dialog's name says what closes.
  • Repeated actions are text buttons, not icons. The image manager says "Move up", "Move down" and "Remove", with the object in a visually hidden span: "Move up: test shot 1" reads as "Move up: test shot 1".
  • External links say so in words. The arrow is decorative; a visually hidden "(opens in a new tab)" follows the link text: "Book on the studio's site (opens in a new tab)".
  • Status icons never stand alone. The alert title, toast title or badge says the same thing in words (WCAG 1.4.1).
  • The play button names the episode. "Play episode" is visible and a hidden ": Episode 4, The Junction Loft" completes the name.
  • ShotThere has no tooltips. On touch there is no hover, so an icon-only control's name must already be clear without one.

Usage

  • Use the icon from this sheet whose name matches the action. If none matches, write the label without an icon rather than borrowing a near miss.
  • One icon per button. Leading icons sit before the label ("Copy link", "Try again"); the external-link arrow trails, because it points where the link goes.
  • No icons in headings, in body copy, in the specification lists or as bullets. Photos and plain headings carry the review.
  • Never draw a score, a light level, a permit status or a stale flag as an icon alone. Scores are a number with pips, permits are "Required" or "Not required", stale is "Prices may be out of date".
  • Keep icons the colour of their text. An icon sits in a tinted circle only in an empty state or a dialog's status icon.

Accessibility

  • Non-text contrast (1.4.11). Icons that carry meaning keep 3:1 against their background in both themes. Decorative icons beside text inherit the text colour, which already passes 4.5:1.
  • Text alternatives (1.1.1). Decorative icons are aria-hidden="true"; icon-only controls have aria-label.
  • Forced colours. Because icons draw with currentColor, Windows High Contrast recolours them with the surrounding text automatically. The filled play triangle uses currentColor too.
  • Consistent identification (3.2.4). One glyph, one meaning: the triangle only ever means "warning", the external arrow only "leaves ShotThere".
