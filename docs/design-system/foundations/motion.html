Foundation

Motion

Motion in ShotThere is quiet and quick. Overlays rise or scale into place, photos lean in a little when you point at them, and nothing moves on its own. It shows where something came from and never makes anyone wait to read a price or a score.

Overview

Every transition and animation in components.css reads a --duration-* and an --ease-* token. Nothing hard-codes a time. That gives the site one rhythm, and it makes reduced motion a token change: prefers-reduced-motion: reduce sets the transition durations to 0.01ms in tokens.css, so components need no media queries of their own.

  • Short. Hover and press changes take 120ms; toasts and the lightbox fade 200ms; the filters sheet, confirmation dialogs and the photo zoom 320ms. Nothing that blocks input runs longer than 320ms.
  • Purposeful. Motion says where something came from: the filters sheet rises from the bottom edge on a phone and slides in from the right on a tablet; a confirmation such as "Unpublish The Junction Loft?" scales up from the centre; the map popup grows out of its marker.
  • Calm. No bounce, no parallax, no autoplay. The lightbox changes photo only when someone presses Next or Previous, and the episode player loads only after Play episode (L2-047).

Each button below replays a demo. With reduced motion turned on in your operating system the dot jumps straight to the end, which is exactly what the product does.

Durations

Five transition durations cover every state change. Pick by distance and size: small, local changes are fast; big surfaces that enter the screen are slow.

All five use --ease-standard. Each demo sets --demo-duration to the token it names.

Easing

Things that arrive decelerate into place (--ease-enter); things that leave accelerate away (--ease-exit); things that change in place use --ease-standard. The demos run at 500ms so the curves are easy to see; in the product each easing pairs with the duration in the table above.

Leaving is quicker than arriving, or instant: once someone has decided to close the filters or cancel an unpublish, nothing should hold them up. Stacked toasts ("Saved with a new address" above "Corktown Garage Studio is published") each slide up on their own; they never animate as a group. The compare bar stays put on purpose: it is a persistent tool, and a sliding bar under the thumb would move the Compare button just as someone reaches for it.

Usage

  • Always write transition: <property> var(--duration-*) var(--ease-*). Never a literal time, never all: list the properties that change.
  • Animate opacity, translate, scale, rotate and colours. Never animate width, height, top or margin on large elements; the exceptions are the upload progress bar width and the skip link's top.
  • Use a CSS animation for entrances (it plays when the element appears) and a transition for state changes (it follows the state both ways, like the compare toggle and the editor-section chevron).
  • New loops need a reason a person cares about ("still loading", "still uploading") and must stop under reduced motion unless they report status.
  • Nothing moves on its own: no auto-advancing lightbox, no autoplaying episode, no wiggling "Book on the studio's site" button, no animated count in the compare bar.
  • Never delay content to show an animation. Review pages are server-rendered (L2-074); skeletons replace content only while a list is genuinely loading.

Accessibility: reduced motion

With prefers-reduced-motion: reduce, animations are turned off or reduced to an instant change (WCAG 2.3.3). The system meets it in two layers.

  1. Tokens. tokens.css sets --duration-fast, --duration-base, --duration-slow and --duration-deliberate to 0.01ms. Every transition and every entrance animation that reads them becomes instant. (0.01ms rather than 0, so animationend and transitionend still fire for scripts that wait for them, such as the one that moves focus into the lightbox.)
  2. Components. The motion block in components.css stops what the tokens cannot reach, because loops keep their own durations.

Test it: in Chrome DevTools open Rendering and set "Emulate CSS media feature prefers-reduced-motion" to reduce, then open the studio filters, a confirmation dialog, the lightbox and the map popup, copy a comparison link to raise a toast, and hover a studio card. Nothing should slide, scale, glide or shimmer, and the Save changes spinner should still turn.

Other motion rules: nothing flashes more than three times a second (2.3.1); nothing moves for more than five seconds on its own except loading indicators, which end when loading ends (2.2.2); motion never carries meaning alone, so a toast is also announced by its live region, the lightbox count ("Photo 3 of 8") is a polite live region, and a loading list carries aria-busy="true".
