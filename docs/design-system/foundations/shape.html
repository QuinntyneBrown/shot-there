Foundation

Shape

Rounded rectangles for photos and panels, pills for anything you press, and two marker shapes on the map. Six radii and two border widths cover every corner and edge.

Overview

A ShotThere photo shows a room or a place, and its corners matter: where the windows are, how far the cyc runs. So photos stay rectangles with softened corners, never circles. Controls are pills, panels have generous corners, and shape carries meaning in exactly one place: on the map, a studio is a square and a spot is a circle.

The rule: the radius grows with the element. A 24px badge is a pill, a 44px input has 12px corners, a test shot 12px, a location card photo and the summary panel 20px, a dialog rising as a phone sheet 28px. Small radii on big panels look stiff; big radii on small boxes turn them into blobs.

Photos and media

Every photo is a rectangle at a fixed aspect ratio, cropped with object-fit: cover inside a box that clips to its radius. The ratio is fixed per slot so a list of seven studios lines up and a slow image never shifts the layout; until it loads the slot shows --color-bg-photo.

  • Nested corners shrink. A thumbnail inside a table row (md) is sm; a photo inside a 20px panel would be md. The gap between the two curves stays even.
  • Hover scales the image, not the frame. Location card and shot images scale to 1.04 inside their clipped box; the box itself never moves.
  • The focus outline follows the frame. The location card draws its outline at --radius-lg, 8px outside; shots and the poster keep their own radius on focus.
  • Failed images keep their shape. .media-fallback fills the same box with the alt text (L2-086.1).

Map markers and pips

On the map, shape is the type: a studio marker is a 24px square with --radius-xs, a spot marker a 24px circle with --radius-full. The legend keys repeat the shapes at 16px. Both markers sit in a 44px round hit area (--target-comfortable), which also takes the focus outline.

Border widths

Two widths. Most edges are a 1px hairline; 2px is for marks that must read as a line rather than an outline.

Focus rings are not borders: they are a 2px outline with a 3px offset, owned by the Accessibility foundation, and follow the element's radius.

Usage

  • Pick the radius by element from the table above; do not choose by taste per component.
  • Pressable means pill. Buttons, the compare toggle, chips and tags are always --radius-full. A rectangular button reads as an input.
  • Photos are rectangles. Always at their slot's aspect ratio, cropped with object-fit: cover, and clipped to the slot's radius.
  • Shape means type only on the map. Square is studio, circle is spot. Do not reuse those two shapes for anything else on the map screens.
  • Square against an edge. A sheet, bar or full-bleed map that touches the screen edge has square corners on that side.
  • Hairline by default. Use thick only for marks that carry state (the "Draft" ring, the open marker) or that must read as a line (the disclosure stripe).

Accessibility

  • Shape never carries meaning alone. Every marker has an accessible name that says its type ("The Junction Loft, studio"), and the legend names both shapes in text (L2-027.1). Status labels pair a filled or hollow dot with a word (1.4.1).
  • Edges hold 3:1. A pill control's outline and an empty pip's ring use --color-border-strong (3:1 on every surface, 1.4.11, L2-068.1); radius does not lower that requirement.
  • Targets are measured by their box. A map marker's 24px mark sits in a 44 × 44px hit area; never shrink the hit area to the visible shape.
  • Forced colours. Buttons, inputs, cards, chips, badges and tags get a 1px CanvasText border in forced-colours mode, so rounded controls keep a visible edge when backgrounds are removed; markers keep their shapes.
  • Crops must not hide what the alt text describes. The admin writes alt text for the full photo ("two tall north windows over a brick wall, a white cyc in the right corner"); a 3:2 crop must keep those things in frame.
